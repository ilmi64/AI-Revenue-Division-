## 3. Detailed evidence and limitations

### 3.1 BOSS

Official sources:
- https://www.boss.dev/doc/
- https://www.boss.dev/doc/get-started
- https://www.boss.dev/

BOSS documentation lists Canada among countries whose legal residents may earn bounties. Earners must be individuals rather than organizations/businesses, and need a GitHub account, BOSS account, bank account and personal details for financial regulations. Bounties are earned by closing an issue with a BOSS bounty attached; the documentation describes automatic bank deposit where the repository integration supports it.

Limitations:
- This is platform-level eligibility, not confirmation that the owner has registered or completed identity/payment setup.
- The exact task must visibly have an active bounty attached. A normal GitHub issue or request to fix something is not a paid bounty.
- The repository owner controls whether a PR is accepted/merged. No payout is guaranteed until the platform's qualifying completion condition is met.
- Complete net payout fees for a Canadian payout were not established from the production documentation reviewed. Confirm current fees and currency conversion before any claim.
- BOSS currently pays individuals, not the AI Revenue company. Any participation would be in the owner's personal capacity and subject to platform terms and applicable tax obligations. Do not represent the company as payee.

### 3.2 GitHub Bug Bounty

Official sources:
- https://bounty.github.com/rewards
- https://bounty.github.com/rules.html
- https://bounty.github.com/ineligible
- https://www.hackerone.com/policies/code-of-conduct
- https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/8395720-payment-preferences

GitHub's published public reward guidelines are USD $250 low, $2,000 medium, $5,000 high and $10,000 critical. GitHub says these are general guidelines, not guaranteed payouts; final reward depends on demonstrated impact, exploitability, exposure and mitigating controls. Duplicate reports are paid only to the first qualifying report.

GitHub's rules require exact in-scope targets and prohibit social engineering, phishing, physical attacks, DDoS/volumetric attacks, excessive automated traffic and intentional access to other people's personal information. Its safe-harbour statement applies only to research consistent with its policy and cannot bind third parties.

HackerOne's current Code of Conduct permits responsible AI assistance but requires human validation and prohibits fully autonomous hackbot operation. All AI-assisted activity remains subject to programme scope, automation limits and safety restrictions. Reports must be accurate, reproducible and demonstrate real-world impact.

HackerOne payment documentation requires current identity verification, a valid payment method and an approved tax form. It lists PayPal (no minimum), BTC/USDC wallets (USD $15 minimum) and bank transfer (regular minimum USD $50; priority USD $100), with documented processing windows. Actual eligibility, rail availability, bank acceptance, currency conversion and fees must be confirmed in the owner's payment setup before relying on a payout.

## 5. Execution gates

Before any coding bounty:
1. Capture exact issue URL, current funded amount, currency, funding status and platform bounty identifier.
2. Confirm issue is open, unclaimed/unresolved, no competing PR is likely to win, and repository is active.
3. Read contribution guide, licence, code of conduct, security policy, tests and maintainer acceptance criteria.
4. Verify Canada eligibility, individual payee requirement, payment rail, net fees, tax/KYC requirements and expected payout timing.
5. Estimate effort, review risk and expected value; reject poor economics.
6. Prepare a scoped work plan and exact owner approval packet.
7. Only after owner approval may the owner claim/announce the task or authorize internal implementation; only a separate approval permits public PR submission.

Before any bug bounty work:
1. Verify current programme terms, safe harbor, exact target, test methods, prohibited activity and AI/automation policy.
2. Prefer offline code review, local/sandbox reproduction and researcher-owned accounts.
3. Require separate owner approval for any live test, even if programme policy generally allows it.
4. No credential attacks, phishing, social engineering, persistence, lateral movement, destructive testing, denial of service, data exfiltration, access to real-user data or out-of-scope testing.
5. A human expert must validate every finding and reproduction path. No autonomous testing or submission.
6. No disclosure, report submission or programme contact without separate owner approval.
