# Animora Studio Capability Map

**Status:** Target-state design; not a statement of current staffing, equipment or readiness.

| Capability | Purpose and outputs | Dependencies | Initial delivery approach | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Creative leadership and development | Creative direction, slate, briefs, quality bar, greenlight recommendations | IP register, audience research, budget | Owner-led planning; later accountable creative lead | Scope and rights approved |

| Integrated commerce | Apparel, collectibles, books, digital products and collaborations | Cleared IP, suppliers, unit economics, fulfilment, support | Requirements and supplier research only | Separate funded launch gate |

## Shared governance and handoffs

- Executive Management / OCC owns the programme register, cross-division priorities, approvals and readiness evidence.
- Animora owns creative requirements, canon, approved designs and production acceptance.
- Technology owns secure tooling, storage, version control, backups and technical validation.
- Research owns market, audience, competitor, supplier and tool due diligence.
- Finance owns budget baselines, commitments, cash-receipt reconciliation and cost reporting.
- Risk owns independent challenge on IP, privacy, cybersecurity, contracts, suppliers and continuity.
- Business & Commercial owns proposed partnerships, licensing, distribution and commissioned-work pipelines.
- Ecommerce owns future shop requirements, product economics and fulfilment assessment, but cannot open the store independently of the Animora launch gate.

## Minimum viable future team structure

Roles are capability placeholders, not current appointments:
- Executive/Studio sponsor.
- Creative director / showrunner.
- Producer / production manager.
- Story and script development.
- Character and environment design.
- Animation and compositing.
- Editor and sound lead.
- Technical pipeline / systems lead.
- IP/legal and finance support.
- Audience, partnerships and commerce lead.

Early work may combine roles, but approval, finance and risk challenge should remain independent of the person proposing a commitment. Contractors and collaborators require written scopes and rights terms before work begins.

## Tooling selection criteria

Evaluate tools against:
1. Commercial-use and output rights, including AI-generated content terms.
2. Source-file portability and ability to leave the platform.
3. Project collaboration, versioning and asset interchange.
4. Performance on available hardware and remote workflows.
5. Security, privacy, data retention and model-training settings.
6. Accessibility, learning curve and production-quality limits.
7. Total cost, recurring costs, storage/rendering costs and cancellation.
8. Canadian availability and lawful licensing.

No tool is selected or purchased by this map. Free evaluation only; no billing-risk trial.

## Readiness states

- **Planned:** capability and interfaces described.
- **Building:** workflow, owners, tools or evidence are being developed.
- **Tested:** repeatable workflow has passed documented tests.
- **Standby:** owner, dependencies, recovery and approvals are verified.
- **Operational:** monitoring, reporting, capacity and sustained delivery evidence exist.

A document, model demo, single successful render or repository branch does not prove studio readiness.
