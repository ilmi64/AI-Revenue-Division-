# Animora Creative Production Pipeline

**Status:** Proposed operating workflow. No production is greenlit by this document.

## End-to-end flow

1. **Idea intake**
   - Record concept, audience, format, intended platform, creative owner and source.
   - Identify whether it uses existing Animora IP, new IP or third-party material.
   - Do not promise delivery dates or spend at intake.

2. **Development brief**
   - Define logline, premise, intended audience, format, target runtime, tone, creative goals and success criteria.
   - Identify content sensitivities, accessibility needs and distribution assumptions.
   - Research comparable works and audience evidence without copying protected expression.

3. **IP and rights screening**
   - Check character/world canon, creator contributions, references and embedded materials.
   - Record provenance, ownership basis, licences, releases and restrictions in the IP register.
   - Escalate uncertain rights before further production or public sharing.

4. **Creative greenlight proposal**
   - Producer prepares scope, deliverables, milestones, staffing, dependencies, budget, schedule and risk register.
   - Finance verifies funding availability; Risk independently challenges material risks.
   - Executive owner explicitly approves or rejects the work package. No approval means no commitment.

5. **Script and story development**
   - Create outline, treatment, script, revisions and continuity notes.
   - Track version, author, reviewer and decision history.
   - Lock approved script version for storyboard/animatic work.

6. **Visual development**
   - Prepare character sheets, model sheets, expression/pose guides, environment and prop designs, colour keys and style frames.
   - Validate consistency with the IP bible and approved creative direction.
   - Record source files and rights for every accepted asset.

7. **Storyboard and animatic**
   - Convert approved script into shot sequence, timing, camera and staging.
   - Use temporary audio only where rights are clear or use is internal and appropriately controlled.
   - Review pacing, clarity, runtime and production feasibility before animation.

8. **Production planning**
   - Create shot/asset breakdown, task owners, dependencies, naming conventions, folder structure, backup plan and review schedule.
   - Define acceptance criteria for each shot and final delivery.
   - Confirm funded capacity and contingency before production begins.

9. **Asset and animation production**
   - Produce layout, key poses, animation, cleanup, colour, backgrounds, effects and compositing as appropriate to the chosen style.
   - Maintain versioned project files and daily/periodic backups.
   - Record shot status: queued, in progress, internal review, revision, approved, final.

10. **Editorial and audio post**
    - Assemble picture edit, dialogue, voice, sound design, music, mix, titles and credits.
    - Obtain written permissions/releases for performers, music, fonts, stock and other third-party assets.
    - Keep music and voice rights distinct from visual asset rights.

11. **Quality control**
    - Creative review: story, performance, design consistency and continuity.
    - Technical review: resolution, frame rate, colour, audio levels, sync, subtitles, file integrity and delivery specs.
    - Rights review: credits, releases, licences, trademarks and permitted territories/platforms.
    - Accessibility review: captions/subtitles and other applicable access requirements.
    - Record defects, fixes, approver and final master hash/location.

12. **Release and distribution**
    - Prepare platform-specific masters, metadata, synopsis, artwork, captions and press materials.
    - Confirm distribution rights, platform terms, age/content classifications where applicable and owner approval.
    - Publish only after an explicit release authorization.

13. **Audience and performance review**
    - Measure reach, watch time, completion, engagement, audience feedback, conversion and cost per deliverable where data is lawfully available.
    - Separate observed data from interpretation; small samples do not prove product-market fit.
    - Feed learning into future development without compromising creative direction.

14. **Archive and reuse**
    - Preserve approved masters, project files, scripts, source assets, licences, releases, production logs and delivery records.
    - Maintain redundant backups and test restoration.
    - Record reusable assets and restrictions for future episodes, publishing and merchandise.

## Stage gates

| Gate | Required evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| G0 Intake | Complete idea record and preliminary IP classification | Accept for research or decline |
| G1 Development | Approved brief, audience rationale, format and rights issues identified | Authorize development only |
| G2 Greenlight | Script/creative package, costed scope, funding, owners, schedule, risk review | Authorize bounded production |
| G3 Pre-production | Locked script, approved designs, storyboard/animatic, shot plan, rights and pipeline checks | Authorize production |
| G4 Production review | Shot progress, budget variance, creative and technical reviews | Continue, revise or stop |
| G5 Final acceptance | Creative, technical, accessibility and rights QC complete | Accept final master |
| G6 Release | Distribution rights, metadata, release plan and explicit owner approval | Publish/distribute |
| G7 Closeout | Final costs, receipts, archive, lessons learned and asset register updates | Close project |

## First recommended proof-of-concept format

When funding and approvals permit, begin with a very short original Animora sequence rather than committing immediately to a full episode or film. The exact runtime and style should be selected only after comparing:
- Creative ambition and story requirements.
- Available skill and production capacity.
- Toolchain and hardware performance.
- Cost per finished minute and schedule risk.
- Rights and distribution requirements.

A proof of concept must demonstrate a repeatable pipeline, not merely produce an attractive isolated clip.

## Commerce handoff

Merchandise development is downstream of approved IP and creative design:
- Creative approves canon-consistent design assets.
- IP/Risk confirms ownership and licence scope.
- Ecommerce researches suppliers and obtains samples only after approved funding.
- Finance verifies landed cost, fees, tax assumptions, returns and margin.
- Business & Commercial reviews partnerships/licensing.
- OCC tracks dependencies and launch approval.
- Store opens only at the separate Animora-funded commerce readiness gate.

## Production records

Every project should retain:
- Project ID, title, format, version and owner.
- Brief, script, storyboards and approved designs.
- Contributor list and rights evidence.
- Shot/asset task board and review decisions.
- Budget, commitments, actual costs and variance.
- Risk/incident log and change history.
- QC checklist, final master and distribution approvals.
- Archive location, backup verification and reuse restrictions.

## Current limitations

No production crew, paid tools, hardware, studio budget, legal clearance, production project or release is established by this workflow. The workflow is a planning baseline for later adaptation and rehearsal.
