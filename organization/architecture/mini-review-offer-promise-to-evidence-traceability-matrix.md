# Mini-Review Offer Promise-to-Evidence Traceability Matrix

**Status:** INTERNAL DESIGN / NOT REHEARSED / NOT QA-VALIDATED / NOT PRICED / NOT PUBLISHED.  
**Company:** AI Revenue only. **Budget:** CAD $0 incremental.  
**Authority:** Does not authorize rehearsal, prospect contact, customer-data intake, pricing, proposal, publishing or delivery.

## 1. Purpose

Make every customer-facing promise traceable to a deliverable, evidence source, acceptance test and stop condition before any commercial use. The governing offer remains one workflow, one business unit, one decision-maker, with no production access or implementation.

## 2. Promise-to-evidence matrix

| Offer promise / deliverable | Required artifact | Minimum evidence | Acceptance test | Hard stop / unresolved item |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clear one-workflow boundary | Scope and boundary statement | Named workflow start/end, business unit, decision owner, exclusions | Reviewer confirms one bounded workflow and no hidden second workflow | Unclear ownership, multi-workflow sprawl, production access required |
| Current-state workflow map | Step/role/tool/handoff map | Every step linked to supplied evidence or explicitly labelled statement/assumption | Traceable sequence; waits and handoffs represented; no invented process steps | Missing critical process evidence; sensitive data needed |
| Evidence-led findings | Atomic evidence/claim register | Source/reference, date/as-of, evidence class, author, confidence, contrary evidence | Every material factual claim traceable; client statement distinguished from independently verified fact | Untraceable material claim or false corroboration |
| Baseline snapshot | Baseline table | Aggregate/redacted input, period, units, denominator, data owner and limitations | Recalculate all derived values; label unknowns and sampling limitations | No baseline: do not invent a benchmark or quantify improvement |
| Friction and risk register | Ranked friction/risk items | Evidence, impact pathway, likelihood/impact rationale, owner/control dependency | Each item traces to a map step and is not stated as proven without evidence | Material risk outside competence or regulated/safety-critical domain |
| Three prioritized improvements | Recommendation register | Problem link, proposed change, expected effect, effort, cost, dependency, risk, reversibility | Exactly up to three, ranked with transparent criteria; distinguish recommendation from implementation | Paid dependency, unsafe automation, unsupported benefit claim |

## 3. Evidence classification

Use a closed, visible label for every material statement:

- **OBSERVED_INPUT:** supplied source material or direct observation within the authorized scope; preserve source and time.
- **CLIENT_STATEMENT:** statement attributed to the decision-maker; not independently verified unless separately evidenced.
- **CALCULATION:** reproducible transformation of identified inputs, units and formula.
- **ASSUMPTION:** necessary but unverified premise; include impact if false.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** plausible explanation or expected effect requiring validation.
- **UNKNOWN:** insufficient evidence; do not fill with a guess.
- **EXTERNAL_REFERENCE:** public third-party context, dated and distinguished from the buyer's own baseline.

Multiple models reading the same input do not create independent evidence. A recommendation may be reasonable while its projected benefit remains unproven.

## 4. Release-blocking QA criteria

Any failure in the following blocks release:
1. Correct recipient, version and one-workflow scope.
2. Material claim traceability and evidence-class accuracy.
3. Arithmetic, units, period and formula integrity.
4. Baseline provenance and explicit uncertainty.
5. Privacy, security, rights and data minimization.
6. No unsupported savings, revenue, ROI, accuracy, time or guarantee claims.
7. Independent reviewer and defect closure.

Minor formatting issues may be corrected before release but must still be logged. Scope expansion is not defect correction and requires a new scope decision.

## 5. Time-capture and target reconciliation

The offer specification states a 180-minute target as an unvalidated ceiling. The rehearsal authorization packet and rehearsal plan describe a 210-minute measurement plan. These values are not interchangeable and neither is a validated delivery promise.

Before any authorized rehearsal, the owner/reviewer must choose one of:
- **A — 210-minute measurement budget:** retain the existing 210-minute phase plan and treat 180 minutes only as a stretch hypothesis; or
- **B — 180-minute measurement budget:** revise phase allocations and remove/reduce scope transparently before the exercise.

Do not silently compress QA, evidence capture, privacy checks, reviewer independence, admin or closeout to meet a headline target. Capture active labour and elapsed waiting separately; include acquisition, qualification, proposal, setup, delivery, QA, rework, support, admin and closeout in full-cycle economics.

## 6. Commercial evidence gates

A positive synthetic rehearsal can validate only the internal method under fictional inputs. It cannot establish:
- customer demand or willingness to pay;
- real buyer acquisition cost or conversion;
- actual production delivery time;
- customer outcome or realized savings;
- legal/tax/privacy compliance or commercial viability.

Before pricing or selling: two separately authorized synthetic rehearsals; contemporaneous time and cost capture; independent QA; comparative review; owner-approved shadow labour rate and economic floor; base/stress economics; buyer validation through separately authorized means; and legal, tax, privacy, contract, retention and payment review.
