# AI Revenue — Prospect Qualification and Validation Framework

**Status:** Internal preparation; no prospect list assembled, no contact made, no customer demand validated.  
**Company:** AI Revenue Company only.  
**Authority:** External validation, outreach, publishing, proposals, contracts and customer data access require separate explicit owner approval.

## 1. Purpose

Prevent the company from selling generic AI services to unqualified buyers. Qualify a specific workflow problem before proposing a mini-review, and distinguish market interest from a real buying signal.

## 2. Ideal initial prospect profile (hypothesis)

Prefer a small Canadian business where:
- one recurring administrative or customer-intake workflow is clearly identified;
- the workflow is frequent enough to matter;
- the owner or responsible manager can explain the current process;
- the business can provide non-sensitive baseline counts and time estimates;
- the decision-maker can approve a bounded diagnostic;
- an initial process improvement may be possible without paid software or production access.

Potential workflow examples: enquiry intake, quote follow-up, appointment preparation, document routing, internal status updates or repetitive reporting. These are discovery examples, not a claim that any sector has validated demand.

## 3. Qualification rubric

Score each dimension 0–2. A score is a triage aid, not a statistical prediction.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem clarity | No defined problem | General frustration | One workflow and failure mode identified |
| Frequency / impact | Rare or unknown | Some evidence | Repeated and measurable |
| Baseline evidence | None / unwilling | Approximate | Non-sensitive sample or reliable measures |
| Decision authority | No access | Influencer only | Decision-maker engaged |
| Scope fit | Multi-system transformation | Some boundaries | One workflow / one unit |
| Data suitability | Sensitive / unclear | Needs review | Minimal non-sensitive evidence sufficient |
| Buyer urgency | No priority | Exploring | Time-bound operational need |
| Commercial readiness | Wants free custom work | Interested, no budget clarity | Understands paid diagnostic and approval process |

Suggested internal routing:
- 13–16: qualified for an owner-approved paid-diagnostic discussion;
- 9–12: nurture / clarify only, no custom analysis;
- 0–8: decline or defer.

Hard-stop overrides any score: requests for credential sharing, unauthorized access, guaranteed savings, prohibited data handling, unpaid custom consulting, unsafe or regulated decision automation, or work requiring spending/production access not approved.

## 4. Qualification conversation — short and non-consultative

The free conversation is for fit, not a free audit. Ask:
1. Which single workflow is causing the most avoidable delay or rework?
2. How often does it happen, and who owns it?
3. What is the current process from start to finish?
4. What evidence can be shared without personal, confidential or sensitive data?
5. What happens when the process fails or is delayed?
6. Who approves a paid review and what decision would the review support?
7. Is the business seeking diagnosis only, or expecting implementation?

Do not design the solution, inspect systems, calculate bespoke ROI or deliver a custom roadmap during the free qualification conversation.

## 5. Evidence standard

Every note must label each statement as:
- **Observed:** directly evidenced in an authorized sample;
- **Client-reported:** stated by the prospect, not independently checked;
- **Calculated:** arithmetic from identified inputs;
- **Assumption:** used because evidence is missing;
- **Unknown:** not established.

Do not collect names, customer records, credentials, account exports or personal data for initial qualification. Use aggregate counts and redacted examples where possible. Do not place prospect information in Trading company records or unrestricted shared Research memory.

## 6. Validation signals

Weak signals:
- likes, views, compliments, survey hypotheticals;
- free discovery calls with no defined workflow;
- requests for a free custom audit;
- a prospect saying AI is interesting.

Stronger signals:
- a decision-maker describes a specific recurring workflow and its impact;
- the prospect can provide a safe baseline;
- the prospect understands the bounded paid deliverable and exclusions;
- the prospect asks about scope, timing, price or procurement;
- a paid engagement is explicitly approved, contracted and paid.

Only collected and reconciled funds count as revenue. Pipeline estimates, verbal interest, unsigned proposals and unpaid invoices are not revenue.

## 7. Prospect validation plan — approval gated

Before any external validation:
1. Define the target sector and prospect criteria.
2. Prepare a neutral one-page explanation of the diagnostic.
3. Prepare a short qualification script and data-minimization notice.
4. Define the exact channel, recipient set, message, frequency and stop conditions.
5. Confirm applicable Canadian anti-spam, privacy, consumer, contract and tax obligations with qualified sources/advice.
6. Present the complete proposed outreach action to the owner for explicit approval.

No scraping personal contact details, bulk unsolicited messaging, automated outreach, account registration or public claims without separate approval. Do not contact any prospect under this framework until authorized.

## 8. Disqualification and referral

Decline or refer work that requires:
- legal, tax, accounting, medical or regulated professional advice;
- access to sensitive personal or confidential data that cannot be safely minimized;
- security certification or penetration testing;
- autonomous decisions affecting rights, eligibility, credit, employment or safety;
- guaranteed ROI, revenue, lead volume or hours saved;
- unsupported production integration, hosting or ongoing service levels;
- paid services or customer commitments outside approved scope.
