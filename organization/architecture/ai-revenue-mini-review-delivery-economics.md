# AI Revenue — Mini-Review Delivery Economics

**Status:** Internal planning model only; no price approved, no customer demand validated, no delivery-time evidence collected.  
**Owner:** AI Revenue Company.  
**Budget:** CAD $0 incremental by default.  
**Related:** `workflow-mini-review-template.md`, `sample-synthetic-workflow-mini-review.md`, `revenue-first-commercialization-plan.md`.

## 1. Decision

Keep the mini-review as the leading paid diagnostic hypothesis, but do not publish a price or quote a prospect yet. The existing 180-minute delivery target is a ceiling to test, not evidence that the work can reliably be completed in three hours.

The benchmarked C$500–C$750 mini-review price is one competitor's advertised range, not an AI Revenue price recommendation. A price must be supported by measured delivery effort, a defined buyer outcome, direct costs, support/rework allowance and willingness-to-pay evidence.

## 2. Define the true delivery unit

The report template's 180-minute allocation covers:
- preparation and scope: 20 minutes;
- workflow review: 60 minutes;
- analysis and prioritization: 45 minutes;
- report preparation: 45 minutes;
- QA and handover: 10 minutes.

This is incomplete as a commercial unit unless the following are separately measured:
- lead qualification and sales time;
- scheduling and administration;
- proposal and engagement setup;
- client clarification and waiting;
- revisions and handover;
- payment and bookkeeping administration;
- data handling, retention and deletion;
- rework caused by unclear evidence or scope;
- support after delivery (currently excluded).

Record both **hands-on minutes** and **elapsed calendar time**. Do not hide acquisition or administration time simply because it occurs outside the report itself.

## 3. Cost model

For each engagement calculate:

`Total labour hours = (delivery + sales allocation + administration + QA + rework) / 60`

`Labour cost = total labour hours × internal shadow hourly rate`

`Contribution before tax = collected fee excluding applicable sales tax − payment fees − direct delivery costs − allocated support/rework costs`

`Economic contribution = contribution before tax − labour cost`

`Contribution margin = contribution before tax / collected fee excluding applicable sales tax`

The internal shadow hourly rate is a management assumption used to avoid treating founder time as free. It is not necessarily an immediate cash wage. Run several owner-approved internal scenarios rather than selecting a convenient rate.

Include:
- payment processor fees and chargebacks;
- any paid model, software, hosting or storage (default zero; never incur without approval);
- HST/accounting treatment confirmed by qualified advice;
- refunds, rework and non-payment reserve;
- customer acquisition effort and any permitted channel costs;
- currency conversion if any;
- privacy/security handling and insurance/legal costs where applicable.

Do not treat HST collected as revenue. Confirm registration and tax obligations before invoicing; do not infer tax treatment from this planning model.

## 5. Scope controls needed to protect the cap

The mini-review is limited to:
- one workflow;
- one business unit;
- one decision-maker;
- one defined start/end boundary;
- a short evidence set supplied or authorized by the client;
- three prioritized opportunities;
- one concise report and one handover;
- no implementation, integrations, account access, production change or customer messaging;
- no legal, tax, accounting or security certification;
- no guaranteed ROI or savings;
- no unlimited revisions or ongoing support.

If the workflow is regulated, safety-critical, involves sensitive personal information, has unclear ownership, or cannot be assessed without production access, decline or defer it pending qualified review.

## 6. Synthetic rehearsal protocol

A future rehearsal may use only the existing fictional Northstar example and synthetic inputs:
1. Start a timer for each activity category.
2. Record every clarification, assumption and rework event.
3. Produce the report using the existing template.
4. Have an independent reviewer check factual separation, scope, arithmetic, privacy and clarity.
5. Record total hands-on time, elapsed time, defects, rework and unresolved questions.
6. Repeat with a different synthetic workflow before treating the estimate as repeatable.

This is a proposed protocol. No rehearsal or test has been performed or authorized.

## 7. Pricing decision gates

Do not set a price until all are true:
- a narrow deliverable and exclusions are stable;
- at least two synthetic rehearsals have measured effort and QA outcomes;
- a credible delivery-cost model includes founder labour and rework;
- the company can explain the buyer outcome without promising savings;
- a lawful, owner-approved validation method has produced real buyer feedback;
- payment, tax, refund, data-handling and contract responsibilities are understood;
- the proposed fee passes the base and stress-case economic floor.

A competitor's advertised price is context only. It is not a floor, ceiling or proof of willingness to pay.
