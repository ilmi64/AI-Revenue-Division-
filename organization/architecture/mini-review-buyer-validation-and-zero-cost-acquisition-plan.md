# Mini-Review Buyer Validation & Zero-Cost Acquisition Plan

**Status:** PREPARED — NO OUTREACH, PUBLICATION, ACCOUNT CREATION, OR CUSTOMER CONTACT AUTHORIZED  
**Offer:** Practical Operations Improvement Mini-Review  
**Commercial posture:** Validate a narrow buyer problem before committing to price, delivery promise, or scale. CAD $0 incremental spend.

## 1. Validation objective

Determine whether a clearly defined buyer segment experiences a recurring, costly workflow problem that they would consider paying to have mapped and improved. This plan does not assume demand, conversion, willingness to pay, or delivery economics.

## 2. Initial buyer hypotheses (rank for investigation, not claims)

1. Small service businesses handling enquiries, quotes, bookings, and follow-ups through fragmented email, forms, spreadsheets, and messaging.
2. Small agencies or independent operators handling client onboarding, approvals, document collection, and status updates.
3. Small professional-service teams repeatedly transferring information between intake, scheduling, billing preparation, and customer updates.

**Initial wedge:** One repeatable workflow, one accountable owner, one measurable baseline, one concise evidence-backed review. Do not market as a broad AI transformation or promise savings before measurement.

## 3. Problem evidence to collect (only through separately authorized, lawful means)

For each candidate segment, record:
- exact workflow and trigger;
- roles involved and handoffs;
- weekly/monthly volume;
- elapsed time and active labour time, kept separate;
- rework, missed follow-up, delay, error, and exception frequency;
- systems and data categories involved;
- current workaround and why it persists;
- cost of the problem as stated by the buyer, with provenance;
- existing alternatives and why they are insufficient;
- urgency, budget authority, procurement constraints;
- privacy, confidentiality, retention, and security constraints.

Label every item OBSERVED_INPUT, CLIENT_STATEMENT, CALCULATION, ASSUMPTION, HYPOTHESIS, UNKNOWN, or EXTERNAL_REFERENCE. Do not convert a buyer statement into a verified outcome.

## 4. No-cost channel hypotheses

Evaluate, but do not activate without approval:
- direct professional network and warm introductions;
- relevant local business/community groups where commercial solicitation is permitted;
- public founder/operator communities with explicit service-offer rules;
- a simple evidence-led sample report or synthetic case study;
- a concise service page once hosting/contact details and publication are authorized;
- referral relationships with bookkeepers, web developers, and small-business support providers, subject to permission and conflicts checks.

No paid ads, lead lists, scraping, mass messaging, unsolicited automation, platform-rule evasion, or claims of guaranteed savings.

## 5. Interview and validation design

A future owner-approved discovery conversation should ask about the last real occurrence, not hypothetical enthusiasm:
1. Walk through the most recent instance from trigger to completion.
2. Where did work wait, get re-entered, or need correction?
3. How often did that happen in the last 30 days?
4. What records support the estimate?
5. What has already been tried?
6. What happens if nothing changes?
7. Who owns the workflow and approves spending?
8. What would make an outside review safe and useful?
9. What would prevent purchase or implementation?
10. What would a credible, acceptable first engagement look like?

Do not ask for confidential records during discovery. Do not collect personal or customer data unless a separate lawful, secure process is approved.

## 6. Evidence thresholds before commercial claims

- Problem prevalence: multiple independent accounts within the same segment, with denominator and selection bias disclosed.
- Baseline: real workflow evidence, timestamps/volumes where available, owner confirmation, and clear exclusions.
- Benefit estimate: transparent arithmetic with assumptions and sensitivity; no extrapolation beyond evidence.
- Delivery economics: two separately authorized synthetic rehearsals plus contemporaneous time/cost capture and independent QA, followed by owner-approved shadow labour rate and economic floor.
- Price: owner-approved after delivery economics and buyer evidence; no invented market benchmark.
- Outcome claim: only after measured, attributable before/after evidence and permission to use it.

## 7. Selection scorecard

Score each segment 1–5 with evidence notes:
- pain frequency and consequence;
- workflow repeatability;
- access to a decision-maker;
- ability to baseline without sensitive data;
- delivery fit with existing templates;
- privacy/security complexity (reverse score);
- sales-cycle friction (reverse score);
- plausible budget and willingness-to-pay evidence;
- ability to deliver value without paid tools or production integration.

Choose the narrowest segment with the strongest evidence, not the largest theoretical market.

## 8. Stop conditions

Stop or redesign if:
- workflow depends on regulated advice or licensed activity outside approved scope;
- customer data cannot be handled lawfully and securely;
- buyer requires guaranteed ROI, unsupported claims, or unapproved integrations;
- delivery requires paid APIs, hosting, subscriptions, credits, or other spend;
- no measurable baseline can be established;
- scope cannot be bounded to one workflow;
- economics fail the owner-approved floor;
- buyer expects implementation or production access beyond the approved offer.

## 9. Immediate preparation tasks (no external activation)

- Prepare one synthetic case study clearly marked fictional.
- Prepare a one-page discovery note and evidence capture sheet.
- Prepare a buyer-segment comparison sheet.
- Prepare a service-page copy draft with no unsupported claims.
- Keep Taskly as a conditional supplementary acquisition route only; verify exact checkout amount before any owner-authorized application or identity step.
- Continue software/automation opportunity research in parallel; do not make the Mini-Review the sole revenue path.

## 10. Authorization boundary

This plan authorizes no outreach, interviews, account creation, publication, submissions, customer contact, data collection, contract, invoice, payment, or external commitment. Each requires explicit owner approval and a defined scope.
