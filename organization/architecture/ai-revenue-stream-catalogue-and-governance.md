# AI Revenue Company — Revenue Stream Catalogue & Governance

**Date recorded:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** Owner-agreed company-wide scope; individual offers and execution remain readiness- and approval-gated.  
**Budget:** CAD $0 incremental by default.  
**Purpose:** Preserve every material revenue family discussed before and after the expanded company structure, without confusing a candidate with an active offer or verified income.

## 1. Company-wide mandate

AI Revenue is a broad AI-operated technology and commercial company. It may earn by doing client/bounty work, by building owned products and intellectual property, and by operating repeatable managed or subscription services. The division labels below organize capabilities; they do not narrow or replace the underlying revenue opportunities.

### Revenue engines

1. **Earn by doing:** commissioned software and website work, coding issues, paid bug bounty/security research within authorized scope, automation implementation, creative production, SEO/content services, research reports and other accepted project work.
2. **Earn by building:** SaaS, apps, websites/platforms, developer tools, reusable automation, digital templates/guides/assets, software libraries and other owned IP.
3. **Earn by operating:** managed automation, AI customer-service and business workflows, recurring content/SEO/marketing operations, maintenance/support retainers, subscriptions and other repeatable services.

## 2. First-class divisions and revenue families

### B. Coding Bounties & Technical Challenges
- Paid GitHub issues and feature/bug-fix opportunities.
- Open-source bounty and sponsorship programmes.
- Developer challenges, hackathons and competitive programming where prizes and rules are clear.
- Smart-contract/blockchain development and audit challenges within authorized scope.
- AI, data, machine-learning and model-evaluation challenges.
- Responsible vulnerability disclosure and paid bug bounty programmes, including Meta's programme and other eligible platform/vendor programmes.

#### Bug bounty and vulnerability research controls
- Maintain a programme-specific registry of assets, exact scope, exclusions, test methods, rate limits, safe-harbour terms, disclosure rules, eligibility, payout conditions and prohibited actions.
- Use only expressly authorized programme scope and permitted test methods. No arbitrary scanning, exploitation, persistence, disruption, data extraction, real-user account access or testing outside scope.
- Prefer source review, local labs, supplied test environments and low-impact validation. Minimize access and stop immediately on unexpected sensitive data or service impact.
- Validate reproducibility and impact; distinguish suspected issues, duplicates, known issues, out-of-scope findings and confirmed vulnerabilities.
- Reports must be factual, concise, reproducible, safely evidenced and independently reviewed. Never fabricate findings or use AI-generated speculation as proof.
- Check researcher/jurisdiction eligibility, identity/KYC requirements, tax implications, payout minimums, reward discretion, duplicate rules, disclosure deadlines and programme terms before investing effort.
- No paid tools, accounts, infrastructure, API usage or testing costs without explicit owner approval.

#### General bounty controls
- Verify acceptance criteria, claimant eligibility, competition, payout reliability, dispute/duplicate rules, deadlines, licensing and IP assignment before selection.
- Estimate probability-adjusted expected value against effort, opportunity cost, technical risk and payment delay; reject negative or opaque economics.
- No fake identities, fabricated work, manipulated contests, plagiarism, licence violations or unauthorized access.
- Submission, disclosure, customer/programme contact and acceptance of terms remain owner-approval gated.

## 4. Opportunity lifecycle and evidence

All opportunities use one traceable pipeline:

**Discover → Register → Verify eligibility and terms → Qualify economics/risk → Prioritize → Owner-approved selection → Internal execution → Independent QA → Owner-approved external submission/delivery → Acceptance verification → Payment verification and reconciliation → Post-mortem and learning.**

Each opportunity record should include:
- Unique opportunity ID, source, discovery date, expiry/deadline and source evidence.
- Company/division owner, buyer/programme, jurisdiction and eligibility.
- Scope, deliverables, exclusions, acceptance criteria, IP/licence and disclosure terms.
- Required skills, estimated effort, tools, dependencies, security/privacy classification and costs.
- Probability-adjusted expected value, payout method/timing, payment reliability and downside.
- Approval state, reviewer, QA evidence, submission/delivery reference and acceptance status.
- Amount advertised, amount claimed, amount accepted, amount payable, amount received and amount cleared—tracked separately.
- Lessons, defects, disputes, rejection reasons and follow-up.

### Revenue accounting vocabulary
- **Opportunity / pipeline:** not revenue.
- **Advertised prize or bounty:** not revenue and not capital.
- **Submitted / under review:** not accepted and not revenue.
- **Accepted / award confirmed:** receivable or expected reward only until payment clears, subject to accounting review.
- **Received but uncleared / reversible:** not treated as cleared cash.
- **Cleared funds:** actual evidenced receipt, recorded in the relevant company's own ledger.
- Never combine gross awards with net proceeds; record fees, withholding, FX, tax and delivery costs separately.

## 5. Priority and launch principle

Prepare the broad company capability, but launch with the earliest independently ready, lawful and economically credible revenue stream(s). Do not wait for every division to be fully built. The practical operations/workflow improvement mini-review remains the existing first-offer preparation track; coding/bounty and other revenue workstreams are added to the company portfolio rather than replacing it. Prioritize opportunities by time-to-first-cleared-revenue, probability of acceptance/payment, zero-upfront feasibility, fit with current capability, delivery effort, legal/security risk and reusable value.

## 6. Absolute governance boundaries

- CAD $0 incremental spend by default. No upfront payments, paid subscriptions, credits, API use, hosting, deployment, purchases, payment-backed trials, deposits, borrowing or leverage without explicit approval.
- No customer/programme contact, proposal submission, bounty submission, vulnerability disclosure, publication, contract, invoice submission, payment action or other external commitment without explicit owner approval.
- No production access, customer credentials, wallet keys, secrets or account connections outside separately approved, least-privilege scopes.
- No unauthorized security testing, exploitation, persistence, data access or disruption.
- Respect laws, programme rules, platform terms, open-source licences, IP ownership, privacy and confidentiality.
- No false claims, fabricated deliverables, deceptive marketing, fake identities, duplicate bounty abuse or manipulated competitions.
- Screen financial/commercial opportunities for relevant halal/Shafi'i considerations; flag uncertainty for qualified review and do not present the catalogue as a religious ruling.
- AI Revenue and Trading & Technology remain independent companies with separate authority, ledgers, data, budgets and launch decisions. No automatic cross-company funding or shared treasury.
- No opportunity becomes an active commitment merely by appearing in this catalogue.
