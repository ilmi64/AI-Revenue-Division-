# AI Revenue — Revenue-First, Zero-Upfront Commercialization Plan

**Status:** Research-backed commercial preparation; no offer has been sold, no customer demand has been validated and no revenue is claimed.  
**Company:** AI Revenue Company only.  
**Planning date:** 2026-11-21.  
**Budget rule:** CAD $0 incremental by default. No paid APIs, hosting, subscriptions, credits, advertising, purchases or billing-risk trials without separate explicit owner approval.  
**Authority:** AI Revenue executives recommend; owner approval remains required for external actions, spending, customer contact, commitments and publishing.

## 1. Executive decision

The company should pursue revenue through a narrow, outcome-based service before attempting inventory ownership, paid software, SaaS development or capital-intensive operations.

Do not sell generic "AI transformation." Sell a specific improvement to an identifiable workflow, with a clear deliverable, bounded scope and measurable acceptance criteria. AI is the production method where useful, not the customer outcome.

Initial thesis:
- Small businesses often already have websites and digital channels, but may not connect them to repeatable sales and operating workflows.
- Time, skills, cost and integration complexity can prevent small businesses from improving existing processes.
- A short, fixed-scope diagnosis and improvement plan can be prepared without paid infrastructure or taking control of a customer's systems.
- Willingness to pay, acquisition cost, delivery time and repeatability remain hypotheses until tested with real prospects and a paid pilot.

## 2. Market evidence and limits

### Statistics Canada — June 11, 2026

The Q2 2026 Canadian Survey on Business Conditions found:
- 19.2% of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services in the prior 12 months, up from 12.2% in Q2 2025 and 6.1% in Q2 2024.
- 40.0% said AI was not relevant to their business.
- Among businesses with 1–4 employees, 19.9% reported AI use and 41.4% said AI was not relevant.
- Among businesses already using AI, the most reported applications were data analytics (36.6%), text analytics (34.5%) and virtual agents/chatbots (28.2%).

Source: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm

Interpretation: there is a growing adoption market, but a generic AI pitch will be irrelevant to many buyers. Qualification must start with a concrete business problem and a buyer who recognizes its cost.

### Statistics Canada — planned use, Q3 2026

25.2% of businesses said they planned to use AI to produce goods or deliver services over the next 12 months, up from 14.5% in Q3 2025.

Source: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026013-eng.htm

Interpretation: planned adoption is a demand signal, not proof that a particular business will buy external services.

### CFIB — December 17, 2025

A survey of 2,478 independent business owners reported:
- 90% use at least one digital channel;
- 78% have a company website;
- 52% use Google Business Profile/Maps and 41% use Instagram;
- 84% use digital channels to promote their business;
- only 33% of business websites are used for direct sales;
- two-thirds of digitally engaged business owners named lack of time as their greatest obstacle.

Source: https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/sme-digital-presence

Interpretation: a basic website is not automatically the strongest first offer. Better opportunities may be improving the path from visibility to enquiries, follow-up, booking, quoting, sales and customer administration.

### BDC — June 2026

BDC reported 96% of Canadian SMEs use at least one digital technology, while only 23% have high or very high digital maturity.

Source: https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/analysis-research/digital-transformation-of-smes-in-the-age-of-artificial-intelligence

Interpretation: the potential gap is integration and effective use, not simply access to another app. These figures do not establish demand for our services or predict conversion.

## 4. First offer specification — Workflow Friction Audit

**Working name:** Practical Operations Improvement Audit.  
**Buyer hypothesis:** owner-operated or small service businesses with recurring enquiries, quoting, bookings, document handling, customer follow-up or repetitive administration.  
**Problem hypothesis:** staff time is consumed by a process that is manual, duplicated, slow, error-prone or difficult to hand off.  
**Customer-facing promise:** a clear, practical map of one workflow and a prioritized plan to improve it. Do not promise a particular savings percentage or autonomous AI system before measuring the process.

### Fixed-scope deliverable

1. One intake interview or written workflow questionnaire (only after customer-contact approval).
2. One current-state workflow map.
3. A list of friction points, handoffs, duplicate entry, delays and avoidable errors.
4. Three prioritized improvements ranked by impact, effort, risk and cost.
5. A conservative time/cost impact model using customer-supplied baseline data and clearly stated assumptions.
6. A privacy/security and data-minimization note.
7. A no-cost / existing-tool option where feasible, plus any paid dependency clearly identified and excluded unless approved.
8. A concise implementation roadmap and acceptance checklist.

### Explicit exclusions for the initial package

- No access to customer systems, inboxes, payment accounts or private datasets unless separately authorized and contractually scoped.
- No installation, production integration, data migration, deployment or ongoing automation.
- No legal, tax, accounting, cybersecurity certification or regulated professional advice.
- No guaranteed savings, revenue uplift, lead volume or AI accuracy.
- No handling of sensitive personal, financial, health or credential data.
- No unlimited revisions or open-ended consulting.

### Evidence required before sale

- A synthetic demonstration using invented data.
- A repeatable template and QA checklist.
- A time-on-task estimate for delivery.
- A cost and gross-margin model with all inference, software, labour, revision and support costs identified.
- A sample report that does not use real customer data.
- A customer acceptance rubric.
- A prospect validation plan approved before any outreach.

## 5. Related offer — Enquiry-to-booking / quote follow-up

This is a potential implementation-oriented follow-on, not an automatic upsell.

Research and document the client's existing path:
1. enquiry source;
2. enquiry capture;
3. qualification;
4. response time;
5. quote or booking;
6. reminder/follow-up;
7. outcome and lost-lead reason;
8. customer handoff.

Possible outputs include response templates, a simple follow-up schedule, a lead-tracking spreadsheet structure, process ownership and a proposed automation design. Any connection to email, CRM, calendar, messaging, booking or payment systems is separately permissioned and may introduce cost. No messages are sent and no records are changed by the audit itself.

## 8. Zero-upfront preparation programme

### Workstream A — offer and proof assets
- Prepare the workflow audit template, questionnaire, process-map notation, impact calculator and report format.
- Build one synthetic example for a fictional small service business.
- Prepare a short service brief, scope, exclusions, acceptance criteria and draft proposal.
- No public posting or customer distribution without approval.

### Workstream B — demand and competitor intelligence
- Identify buyer roles and narrow customer segments.
- Review public competitor offers, deliverables, pricing disclosures and differentiation.
- Distinguish advertised prices from actual transaction evidence.
- Record source URL, access date, geography, offer scope, terms, evidence grade and confidence.
- Avoid collecting personal data or scraping in breach of site terms.
- Do not treat survey-level market evidence as proof of willingness to pay.

### Workstream C — delivery economics
- Time each step of a synthetic delivery.
- Track model/tool use and any cost exposure.
- Estimate labour, revision, support, QA and administration.
- Establish a minimum acceptable gross margin and maximum delivery hours before setting a price.
- If the work requires paid infrastructure, either redesign it for a free/manual delivery path or defer it pending approval.

### Workstream D — validation and first paid pilot
- Prepare a small, owner-approved prospect shortlist using lawful public business information.
- Draft a transparent outreach message and discovery questions.
- Do not contact anyone until explicit owner approval is granted for the exact audience, message and channel.
- Record objections, problem frequency, existing workaround, urgency, decision authority and willingness-to-pay evidence.
- Offer a paid, fixed-scope pilot only after the owner approves the price, scope, terms and delivery commitments.
- Do not provide extensive unpaid custom work as a substitute for validation.
- Count revenue only after payment is actually received and reconciled; distinguish proposal, booked work, invoice, payment pending and cleared funds.

## 9. Opportunity qualification scorecard

Score each offer hypothesis from 0–5 and record evidence, not intuition alone.

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Buyer pain / urgency | 20% | Is the problem frequent, costly and recognized by the buyer? |
| Willingness-to-pay evidence | 20% | Has a qualified buyer indicated budget or accepted a paid pilot? |
| Zero-upfront feasibility | 15% | Can we deliver without new paid tools, hosting, inventory or ads? |
| Time-to-first-cash | 15% | Can the offer be sold and delivered in a short, bounded cycle? |
| Repeatability | 10% | Can templates and QA make the work consistent? |
| Gross-margin potential | 10% | Is price likely to exceed labour, inference, revisions and support? |
| Risk and compliance | 10% | Can privacy, security, legal, platform and delivery risks be controlled? |

A high score is not an automatic go. Critical legal, privacy, safety, security, cost or capability blockers override the numeric score. A paid pilot and actual cleared payment are stronger evidence than survey statistics or competitor claims.

## 10. Commercial funnel and evidence states

Use distinct states:
- IDEA: unqualified hypothesis.
- RESEARCHED: public market and competitor review completed.
- OFFER_DRAFTED: scope, deliverable, exclusions and economics prepared.
- DEMO_READY: synthetic proof asset and QA prepared.
- VALIDATION_AUTHORIZED: owner has approved the exact discovery/outreach action.
- VALIDATION_COMPLETED: evidence from real buyer conversations recorded.
- PILOT_APPROVED: price, scope, terms and delivery approved.
- PILOT_DELIVERED: deliverable completed with acceptance evidence.
- INVOICED: invoice issued through approved process.
- PAYMENT_PENDING: payment not yet cleared.
- REVENUE_RECEIVED: funds received and reconciled.
- REPEATABLE: at least two comparable paid deliveries with measured economics and quality; this is an internal planning threshold, not a guarantee.
- SCALED: capacity, support, risk, cash and quality controls demonstrate safe expansion.

Do not skip states or represent pipeline value as cash.

## 12. First 30-day preparation sequence (not a calendar commitment)

1. Select one narrow workflow and define a synthetic example.
2. Prepare the report template, process map, impact model and QA checklist.
3. Benchmark competitor offers and publicly disclosed prices.
4. Estimate delivery time, cost, revision burden and minimum viable price.
5. Draft one-page service brief and proposal with explicit exclusions.
6. Prepare a prospect qualification rubric and an owner-approval-ready outreach draft.
7. Review the package independently for quality, privacy, security, legal and financial assumptions.
8. Ask for explicit authorization before any external validation or customer contact.

If the first offer fails validation, revise or reject it quickly and test the next offer. Do not build a SaaS product, buy inventory or spend money to rescue a weak hypothesis.

## 13. Launch and revenue truthfulness

This plan is a preparation framework. It does not establish:
- a live company, staffed department or functioning autonomous sales pipeline;
- validated demand, a customer, a signed contract, an invoice or received revenue;
- a deployed product, automation or customer system;
- permission to contact prospects, publish content, accept contracts or spend.

AI Revenue's independent launch gate still requires its own governance, workforce, offer, repeatable delivery, customer operations, finance, security and owner acceptance evidence. A single successful paid pilot is evidence of revenue, not proof that the whole company is operational or ready to scale.
