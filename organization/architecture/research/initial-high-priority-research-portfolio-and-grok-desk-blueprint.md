# Initial High-Priority Research Portfolio & Grok Desk Operating Blueprint

**Status:** Initial prioritized work order; design and public-information preparation only.  
**Owner:** Director of Research & Intelligence.  
**Authority:** Advisory service. Recipient companies retain all commercial, financial, operational and risk decisions.  
**Resource boundary:** CAD $0 incremental spend. No paid APIs, subscriptions, credits, payment-detail trials, installs, executions, tests, external contact, account connections, deployments, publishing or financial actions without separate explicit approval.

## 1. Purpose and operating model

This portfolio converts the existing research architecture into a sequenced work queue. It is deliberately split into three lanes:

1. **AI Revenue Company research** — customer-value and revenue opportunities, delivery economics, product/service validation and AI Creative Studio capability.
2. **Trading & Technology Company research** — crypto/PolyBot first, followed by market-data integrity, quantitative validation, order-flow, Forex, indices and Gold.
3. **Research & Intelligence Division** — the core organization-level shared service that maintains source quality, specialist coverage, capital-flow intelligence, technology intelligence, shift handovers and recipient-scoped publications.

Research & Intelligence is not a third company or an optional future add-on. It is a first-class shared division with its own Director, desk leads, source stewards, analyst/reviewer roles, queues, knowledge governance, audit and continuity design. It is not yet staffed, connected or operational. Its reports are advisory; it cannot authorize company actions.

The four lanes progress in parallel. A shared service, Main Hub/OCC, one company or any particular model provider must not become a launch dependency for either company.

## 2. Scoring and queue rules

Apply the existing **Research Task Prioritization & Opportunity Scoring Protocol**. Scores below are provisional planning estimates to order preparation work, not measured business value, source-quality scores, proof of demand or automatic priority grants.

- P0: security, integrity, privacy, cross-tenant or unexpected-cost incident.
- P1: time-sensitive event with a narrow verification window.
- P2: planned decision-relevant research.
- P3: exploratory work.
- Any unresolved legality, terms/licence, privacy, provenance, recipient scope or cost issue is a hard BLOCK regardless of score.
- Score and urgency are separate. Every task needs a named owner, deliverable, acceptance criteria, review date, evidence grade and recipient.
- Research outputs must distinguish observed facts, official/vendor claims, estimates, hypotheses, reproduced mechanics and independently validated outcomes.
- A score never authorizes outreach, purchases, publishing, software installation, backtesting, paper signals, live trading or other external action.

## 6. Grok Desk / HyperGrok — operating blueprint

### 6.1 Mission and boundaries

Grok Desk is a proposed specialist research desk for rapid discovery, event triage, source comparison and challenge. HyperGrok is the proposed higher-level synthesis/coordination workflow across approved specialist desks. These names describe planned roles, not verified products, connected services, active agents or current operating systems.

The desk may:
- Discover candidate developments from approved public sources.
- Identify changes, contradictions, source gaps and time-sensitive questions.
- Draft evidence-linked research tasks and preliminary summaries.
- Ask a second reviewer/model to challenge assumptions and alternative explanations.
- Prepare recipient-scoped flash alerts, briefs, opportunity dossiers and handovers.

It may not:
- Treat model output, social posts or vendor alerts as facts without source verification.
- access private groups, restricted data, credentials, user accounts or wallets without separately authorized lawful access;
- authorize trades, risk changes, purchases, outreach, publishing, deployment or spending;
- merge the two companies' private context or publish one shared private-context report;
- alter source-quality rules, its own permissions, company policy or approval state.

### 6.2 Proposed desk topology

- **Grok Desk — rapid discovery:** monitors only approved public-source lists when a future approved monitor exists; creates candidate event records with observed time, source identity and confidence.
- **Verification Desk — source and claim checks:** confirms primary-source evidence, timestamps, source revisions, methodology and terms; preserves contradictions.
- **Challenge Desk — independent review:** attempts to falsify the draft, checks alternative explanations, duplicate events, stale evidence and unsupported causal claims.
- **HyperGrok — synthesis coordinator:** groups verified findings by recipient decision, requests specialist input and prepares separate publication drafts. It cannot approve its own material high-impact work.
- **Publication Officer:** validates audience, redactions, expiry, evidence references and delivery envelope before any future authorized publication.
- **Shift Lead:** accepts handover, confirms coverage, prioritizes P0–P3 and records missed coverage or outages.

Model diversity is a review technique, not proof of independence. Two models trained on similar data may repeat the same error. Primary sources and independent human/technical review remain necessary.

### 6.3 Day/night shift cycle

Each shift follows the same bounded cycle:

1. **Start-of-shift:** acknowledge handover; check desk availability, source health, open incidents, due tasks and resource boundary.
2. **Discover:** review only approved source lists and already available public material; record observed time separately from publication time.
3. **Triage:** assign P0–P3, recipient scope, expiry and hard-stop review. Escalate integrity/security incidents through the appropriate company-independent channel.
4. **Verify:** locate primary evidence; check identity, methodology, freshness, terms and corroboration.
5. **Challenge:** document contrary evidence, alternative explanations, uncertainty and what would falsify the claim.
6. **Synthesize:** draft an alert, brief, dossier or source-health notice for exactly one recipient scope.
7. **Review:** independent reviewer checks material claims, source quality, scope and prohibited downstream uses.
8. **Publish/hold:** publication remains DRAFT or QUARANTINED unless a future authenticated, approved publication path exists. Current work stays in prepared documentation.
9. **Handover:** record unresolved items, source failures, next owner and explicit acknowledgement.

### 6.4 Coverage and staffing truthfulness

The target is day/night continuity, not a present 24/7 service. Before any continuous-coverage claim, the organization must demonstrate:
- verified named/agent desk ownership for every shift and fallback;
- approved runtimes, source lists, identity and tenant isolation;
- monitored source ingestion and health alerts;
- time synchronization and freshness/expiry enforcement;
- independent challenge and publication review;
- durable task state, audit, retry/dead-letter and outage recovery;
- acknowledged shift handovers and missed-coverage escalation;
- backup/restore and incident rehearsals;
- recipient-authenticated delivery interfaces.

Until these gates are proven, status must be **PLANNED / NOT OPERATIONAL**. A model being available in a chat product does not constitute unattended monitoring or 24/7 coverage. No Grok API, subscription, account connection or agent deployment is assumed.

### 6.5 Source and evidence record

Every candidate event should capture: event ID; desk; recipient; source canonical ID and URL; publisher; observed and publication timestamps; retrieval/as-of time; source revision/digest when available; claim text; supporting and contradicting evidence; source-quality disposition; evidence grade; confidence rationale; alternatives; freshness expiry; licence/terms; reviewer; next action; and publication status.

Do not infer causation from correlation, whale movement from unlabeled wallet activity, fundraising from an unverified announcement, or market direction from a single flow metric. Preserve uncertainty and missing coverage.

### 6.6 Escalation and outage behavior

- P0: security, privacy, source poisoning, cross-tenant leak, credential exposure or unexpected cost — stop affected work, quarantine and escalate.
- P1: narrow-window material event — rapid verification, but no unreviewed alert.
- Source failure/staleness: mark stale, retain last verified record with its age, pause dependent claims and use only pre-approved fallback sources.
- Model/runtime failure: queue or pause; do not silently switch to an unreviewed provider or broaden permissions.
- Shift missed: record coverage gap; never fabricate a completed scan.
- Delivery/authentication failure: quarantine; no repeated uncontrolled retries or duplicate publication.

## 7. Main Hub / OCC reporting contract

The portfolio is intended to feed the existing Main Hub/OCC through the approved aggregate-only observability boundary. OCC may display:
- work-order ID, recipient, desk, priority and status;
- owner/reviewer role identifiers, due window and next-review timestamp;
- aggregate queue age, workload, completion and blocked counts;
- source-health/freshness bands and coverage gaps;
- incident severity, quarantine state and unresolved blocker counts;
- publication ID/version/status, recipient and expiry;
- evidence references explicitly allowlisted for display.

OCC must not receive raw source-vault contents, another company's private records, customer identifiers, credentials, complete trading strategies or unrestricted report bodies by default. It is read-only: no task reassignment, source authorization, policy override, publication approval, financial action or trading control.

Every Research publication uses the existing Recipient-Scoped Intelligence Delivery Contract: one recipient per publication; separate review and redaction for each company; explicit as-of time, expiry, evidence grades, counter-evidence, source terms, reviewer and prohibited downstream uses. If the same public finding benefits both companies, create two separate publications.
