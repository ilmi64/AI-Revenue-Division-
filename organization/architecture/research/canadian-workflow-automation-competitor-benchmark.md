# AI Revenue — Canadian Workflow Automation Competitor Benchmark

**Research date:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** Public website review only; no provider contacted, no quote requested and no price independently verified.  
**Purpose:** Inform positioning and offer design. Advertised prices are not proof of actual transaction prices, quality, capacity or customer satisfaction.

## 1. Executive finding

A visible Canadian market already exists for workflow audits and automation. Publicly advertised pricing reviewed in November 2026 shows:
- free short discovery calls are common;
- small workflow audits are advertised from roughly C$500 to C$2,400;
- single-workflow implementation commonly starts around C$1,500 and can exceed C$4,900 depending on scope;
- ongoing maintenance/support is separately priced.

This supports testing a narrowly scoped diagnostic service, but it does not prove buyers will pay AI Revenue or that we can profitably deliver the same scope. The market is competitive. We should not compete by promising a cheaper full automation build before we have validated delivery capability, cost, security and support.

## 4. Recommended AI Revenue positioning

Do not lead with "we build AI agents" or "we automate anything."

Position the first offer as:

**A practical, fixed-scope review of one business workflow — showing where time is lost, what evidence supports the finding, and which improvement should come first.**

Differentiators to develop:
1. **Evidence-first:** separate observed facts, client statements, assumptions and recommendations.
2. **No forced software sale:** recommend a process fix or existing-tool improvement before an AI build.
3. **Transparent economics:** show estimated time, direct costs, recurring subscriptions and uncertainty separately.
4. **Client control:** no production changes, customer messages or account access during the audit.
5. **Portable deliverables:** workflow map, findings, options, acceptance criteria and handover document.
6. **Security-aware:** data minimization and no credential collection.

## 5. Proposed commercial ladder — not yet priced

### Stage 0 — short qualification conversation
A short, no-cost conversation to establish whether the business has a specific workflow problem, decision-maker and willingness to share non-sensitive baseline information. Do not perform a custom audit during this call.

### Stage 1 — paid mini-review
A tightly capped review of one process, with a short findings note and three prioritized improvement opportunities. Benchmark context suggests C$500–C$750 is an advertised public range for a mini review by one competitor, not a recommended AI Revenue price. Price must be set only after delivery-time and margin modelling.

### Stage 2 — full workflow audit
A deeper evidence-based workflow map, baseline, root-cause register, options, risk assessment and roadmap. Public comparators advertise C$1,500–C$2,400 for broader audits, but scopes differ. AI Revenue should not copy these prices without validated capacity and a buyer signal.

### Stage 3 — implementation design / build
Only after technical runtime, tool permissions, privacy, security, costs, QA, support and rollback are proven. Any customer-paid software/API subscriptions should be disclosed and held in the customer's own account where appropriate. No implementation is included in the current offer.

### Stage 4 — maintenance
Defer until reliable live workflows exist and support capacity, incident handling, monitoring, service levels and recurring economics have been proven.

## 6. Pricing and margin model to complete before an offer is quoted

For each package calculate:
- discovery and preparation hours;
- workflow analysis and report hours;
- QA/review hours;
- revisions and client handover;
- direct inference/software cost;
- payment processing and tax/accounting cost;
- support and rework reserve;
- customer acquisition effort/cost;
- HST treatment and applicable tax obligations;
- target gross margin and maximum delivery hours.

Formula:
`Contribution = collected fee excluding applicable sales tax - direct delivery costs - payment costs - allocated support/rework costs`

Labour must also be valued, even if founder time is not immediately paid. A package that produces cash but consumes unsustainable delivery time is not a scalable offer.

Do not issue a quote until scope, customer requirements, cost dependencies and approval are known. Do not treat the benchmark range as a price recommendation.

## 7. Risks and safeguards

- Competitor websites may be marketing claims; do not repeat case-study savings as verified facts.
- Free audits can become unpaid consulting. Keep Stage 0 short and qualification-only.
- Avoid implementation promises until the company has proven supported runtimes, secure identity, permission boundaries, monitoring and rollback.
- Do not use customer data in unapproved model or third-party services.
- Do not promise a particular ROI, revenue increase or hours saved without a defensible baseline and measurement plan.
- Customer contact, publishing, proposals, contract acceptance, payment collection and paid delivery require separate owner approval.
